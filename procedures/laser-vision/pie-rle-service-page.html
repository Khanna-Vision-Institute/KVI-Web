Your Questions Answered

What exactly is PIE and how is it different from cataract surgery?
PIE (Presbyopic Implant in Eye) is essentially the same procedure as modern cataract surgery, but performed before a cataract develops. We remove your natural lens that has lost its flexibility and replace it with an advanced artificial lens that provides clear vision at all distances. The key difference is timing - with PIE, you don't wait for cataracts to cloud your vision.
Why did Dr. Khanna write a book about PIE?
After performing thousands of these procedures and witnessing the life-changing results, Dr. Khanna wanted to educate people about this option that many eye doctors don't discuss. "Rejuvenate Aging Eyes: The Miracle of PIE" explains the science, benefits, and patient stories that showcase why this is often the best solution for presbyopia.
Am I too young for PIE?
If you're over 40 and struggling with reading vision, you're likely a candidate. Many patients in their mid-40s choose PIE to avoid decades of reading glasses. The ideal age is typically 45-65, but Dr. Khanna evaluates each patient individually. The key is whether the benefits outweigh staying in glasses.
How long does the PIE procedure take?
The procedure itself takes about 15 minutes per eye. Most patients have both eyes done on separate days, about a week apart. You'll be at our facility for about 2 hours total including preparation and recovery. The results last a lifetime since the implanted lens doesn't age like your natural lens.
What about night driving and halos?
Modern premium lenses have dramatically reduced these issues. During your consultation, Dr. Khanna will discuss your lifestyle and recommend the best lens for your needs. Many patients report better night vision after PIE than they had with their presbyopia and glasses.
Is PIE covered by insurance?
Basic lens replacement may be covered if you have cataracts. For clear lens replacement (RLE/PIE) or premium lens upgrades, insurance typically doesn't cover the procedure. However, we offer flexible financing options, and the long-term savings from not buying glasses often offsets the investment.
What's recovery like?
Most patients see well enough to drive within 2-3 days and return to work within a week. There's no pain, just mild scratchiness the first day. You'll use eye drops for a few weeks. Full visual recovery occurs within 1-3 months as your brain adapts to your new vision system.
