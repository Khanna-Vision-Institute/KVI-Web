The comparison people actually needSMILE vs. SMILE Pro

SMILE vs. SMILE Pro

Same core surgery. Newer laser platform.

Regular SMILE and SMILE Pro are both small-incision lenticule extraction procedures. In each, a femtosecond laser creates a thin lenticule inside the cornea, and the surgeon removes it through a small opening. The “Pro” refers mainly to the newer VISUMAX 800 platform and its digitally assisted workflow.

Your eye is checked, drops and restrictions are reviewed, and someone drives you home. Follow-up is part of the procedure—not an optional add-on.

CandidacyCould SMILE Pro fit your eyes?

Could SMILE Pro fit your eyes?

A good candidate is measured—not guessed.

Current U.S. SMILE labeling is for the reduction or elimination of myopia with or without astigmatism in appropriately selected adults. A comprehensive evaluation is essential because a glasses prescription alone does not reveal whether the cornea is safe for laser correction.

Common candidacy markers

  • Age 22 or older under current U.S. labeling
  • Stable prescription over the required period
  • Eligible nearsightedness, with or without astigmatism
  • Healthy corneal shape and adequate tissue
  • Healthy or adequately treated ocular surface
  • No disqualifying active eye disease
  • Realistic expectations about recovery and results

Why SMILE Pro may not be right—or not yet

  • Age outside current U.S. labeling
  • Unstable prescription
  • Abnormal topography or tomography
  • Keratoconus or increased ectasia risk
  • Significant untreated dry eye
  • Insufficient corneal tissue
  • Percent tissue removal exceeds safe limits
  • Inadequate residual stromal bed
  • Certain eye or systemic health conditions
  • Pregnancy or breastfeeding
  • Expectations no refractive procedure can safely meet

Over 40? The conversation changes

Correcting distance vision does not stop presbyopia—the natural age-related loss of near focus. Dr. Khanna should discuss your reading, computer, night-driving, and long-term lens needs before recommending corneal laser treatment. A distance-only fix can be the wrong answer to a distance-and-near problem.

Not a SMILE Pro candidate does not mean “no options”

Depending on the findings, Dr. Khanna may discuss LASIK or SuperLASIK, ASA/PRK, EVO ICL, a lens-based strategy, continued glasses or contacts, dry-eye treatment before reconsideration, or observation. The newest procedure is useful only when it is the right procedure.

RecoveryQuick for many. Still surgery for everyone.

Quick for many. Still surgery for everyone.

Recovery should fit your life—but it cannot be crowdsourced.

Many patients notice meaningful improvement quickly, sometimes by the next day. Healing speed, comfort, and activity clearance vary. Follow Dr. Khanna’s instructions rather than a generic social-media timeline.

Surgery day

Expect hazy or blurred vision, watering, light sensitivity, mild irritation, or a foreign-body sensation. Use prescribed drops, rest, do not rub the eyes, and have someone drive you home.

First 24 hours

Vision often improves, but may fluctuate. Attend the scheduled follow-up. Return to desk work or screens only as comfort and your surgeon allow.

First few days

Use drops exactly as directed, take screen breaks, blink frequently, wear sunglasses outdoors, and protect the eyes from dust, smoke, and accidental rubbing.

Exercise + water

Light activity may return sooner than strenuous or contact activity. Wait 2–3 weeks for swimming, hot tubs, or oceans. Martial arts, boxing, racquet sports, and other trauma-risk activities require explicit clearance.

Makeup + skincare

Avoid eye makeup and products that could enter or contaminate the eye until cleared. Restart carefully and do not rub eyes.

Driving + travel

Do not drive until you feel comfortable with the vision. Travel may be possible early, but keep drops with you, protect the eyes, and preserve follow-up access.

Screens after SMILE Pro

Phone and computer use does not undo the surgical correction. It can, however, make temporary dryness, fatigue, and blur more noticeable. Use short sessions, frequent blinking, regular breaks, and the drops Dr. Khanna recommends.

Is SMILE Pro “better” than LASIK?

Not universally. SMILE Pro and LASIK use different surgical approaches and each has advantages, limitations, and approved indications. For an appropriate candidate, both can produce strong refractive outcomes. The better procedure is the one that best matches the patient’s anatomy, prescription, ocular surface, lifestyle, and visual goals.

A useful promise

Khanna Vision Institute can promise a careful evaluation and a clear recommendation. We cannot promise perfect vision, zero risk, permanent freedom from glasses, or the same recovery for everyone.

Frequently asked questions

1Is SMILE Pro the same as LASIK?

No. SMILE Pro removes a lenticule through a small incision. LASIK creates a corneal flap and uses an excimer laser to reshape tissue beneath it.

2Does SMILE Pro create a flap?

No flap is created.

3Is SMILE Pro better than regular SMILE?

It uses a newer, faster ZEISS platform with digital alignment and workflow aids. That can improve the treatment experience and workflow, but it does not automatically guarantee a better final visual result.

4How long is the laser step?

ZEISS reports lenticule creation in less than 10 seconds for myopia under specified conditions. The total procedure and appointment take longer.

5Can SMILE Pro correct astigmatism?

It can treat eligible myopia with astigmatism within the applicable U.S. indication and the patient’s measured anatomy.

6Can it correct farsightedness in the United States?

Not under the current FDA approval for VISUMAX 800 with SMILE Pro. International indications can differ.

7Can I work the next day?

Many office-based patients return quickly, but comfort, vision, job hazards, and Dr. Khanna’s instructions determine the timeline.

8Will I never need glasses again?

That cannot be guaranteed. Residual prescription, presbyopia, cataracts, and natural eye changes can create future needs.
