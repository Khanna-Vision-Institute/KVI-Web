Step 1 · Map + plan

We scan your eyes in 3D, check tear film and pupils, and design a custom ablation for day and night vision.

Step 2 · Remove the surface layer

Your surgeon selects the approach that best fits your cornea and goals. All are flap‑free.

ASA technique

Mechanical PRK

Epithelium is gently polished away with a micro‑brush. Then the laser reshapes the cornea.

ASA technique

LASEK

A dilute alcohol ring loosens the epithelium, which is lifted as a sheet and then repositioned after laser treatment.

ASA technique

Epi‑LASIK

A blunt epikeratome separates the epithelial sheet without alcohol and it is replaced after reshaping.

ASA technique

Transepithelial PRK

“No‑touch.” The laser removes the epithelium and performs the treatment in one plan.

Step 3 · Laser reshaping

Modern excimer profiles with appropriately sized optical zones sharpen focus and aim to minimize halos.

Step 4 · Anti‑haze protection

In higher prescriptions, a brief medication may be used to reduce haze risk. Your surgeon personalizes this.

Step 5 · Bandage contact lens

A soft protective lens shields the surface while it heals over the first few days.

Comfort plan

✓

Therapeutic bandage lens for 4–7 days.

✓

Short course of topical drops only as appropriate.

✓

Tailored oral meds to keep the first 48–72 hours manageable.

✓

Chilled tears or cold packs for extra relief.

Great fit if…

  • Corneas are thinner and a flap isn’t ideal
  • Topography is borderline or irregular
  • Contact or impact‑prone sports or jobs
  • Higher dry‑eye risk and you want a flap‑free option

How we decide

Final candidacy follows corneal tomography, tear testing, pupil analysis, and a detailed eye exam.

Goal: safest path to clear vision

Timeline

  • Days 0–2 · Light sensitivity and grittiness are common. Rest and sunglasses help.
  • Days 3–5 · Surface closes for most. Bandage lens removed when healing is complete.
  • Weeks 1–2 · Functional vision for work and driving returns. Halos fade.
  • 1–3 months · Vision polishes to long‑term clarity.

Back‑to‑life guide

  • Office screens: usually 3–5 days
  • Driving: after clearance post bandage removal
  • Eye makeup: 1 week
  • Swimming/hot tubs: 2–3 weeks
  • Contact sports: after surgeon clearance
  • Sunglasses: strongly advised for 3 months midday

What to know

  • Temporary discomfort and light sensitivity are expected early.
  • Recovery is slower than LASIK during week one.
  • Dry‑eye symptoms, halos, or glare can occur during healing.
  • Delayed epithelial healing or corneal haze are uncommon; your plan reduces that risk.
  • Infection and scarring are rare but possible with any surgery.

Is ASA the same as “old PRK”?

No. ASA is a modern, flap‑free family using updated lasers, epithelium techniques, routine bandage lenses, and evidence‑based comfort and anti‑haze strategies.

Which ASA technique is best?

Outcomes are comparable. Your cornea, dryness risk, prescription, and the laser platform guide the choice.

Does ASA hurt?

Expect a few days of discomfort while the surface heals. Today’s bandage lenses, chilled lubrication, and short‑course meds make it manageable for most.

Is the vision as good as LASIK after healing?

Yes for appropriate prescriptions. ASA trades a slower first week for a strong biomechanical profile and no flap.
